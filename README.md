# Wortschatz → C2

An adaptive German vocabulary trainer. It asks for words in both directions (German → English and English → German) and uses spaced repetition, so words you get wrong come back soon and words you know come back less and less often.

## Start

```bash
python3 app.py            # opens http://127.0.0.1:8765 in your browser
python3 app.py --no-browser
```

You only need Python 3. Nothing else has to be installed.

## On your phone

**Recommended: the phone app (Android and iPhone, works offline, no computer needed)**

1. Open **https://rauden0.github.io/german-vocab-trainer/** in the phone's browser.
2. Add it to the home screen:
   - Android (Chrome): menu ⋮ → *Add to Home screen* / *Install app*
   - iPhone (Safari): Share → *Add to Home Screen*
3. It now opens like a normal app and works offline.
   - Progress is stored on the phone.
   - It's the same app, logic and word list as the desktop version.

**Moving progress between the phone and your computer**

In the **Progress** tab on the phone there is a *Backup & sync* section.

- Phone → computer:
  1. On the phone, tap *Export progress.json* and send the file to your computer.
  2. Put it in the project folder, replacing the old one.
  3. Start `python3 app.py`. It merges the file.
- Computer → phone: on the phone, tap *Import progress.json* and pick the `progress.json` from the computer.
- Merging keeps the most recently practised version of each word, so importing twice is harmless.

The phone app is built from the desktop app with `python3 tools/build_web.py` (output in `docs/`, served by GitHub Pages).
Re-run it after changing the word lists, `static/index.html` or `web/engine.js`.

**Alternative: use the desktop app from your phone over Wi-Fi**

- Run `python3 app.py --lan` and open the printed address on your phone.
- The computer must stay on.
- There's no password, so only do this on a network you trust.

## Your progress (`progress.json`)

- The app works from a local database, `german.db`. Git ignores this file.
- After every answer the app also writes **`progress.json`**. It holds your card schedules, your answer history, your settings, and any words you added, edited or deleted.
- On start, the app merges `progress.json` into the database. For each card, the most recently practised version wins.
- **To keep your progress on GitHub:** commit and push `progress.json`.
- **To continue on another computer:** `git pull`, then `python3 app.py`.
- If you practise on two computers without pulling in between, `progress.json` can get a git merge conflict. Pull before you practise.

## How it works

- **Cards:** every word is two cards, DE→EN and EN→DE. Each card has its own schedule.
- **Scheduling (SM‑2):**
  - Wrong answer: the card comes back in about 2 minutes and its "ease" drops.
  - Right answer: the gap grows, from 1 day to 3 days, then weeks, then months.
  - A card counts as **mastered** when its next review is 21 or more days away.
- **Order of cards:** due reviews first, with a few new words mixed in. The number of new words per day is a setting. When nothing is due, you drill your weakest words.
- **Strict German checking:**
  - A missing or wrong article (der/die/das) counts as wrong.
  - A missing `sich` or a small typo counts as "almost".
  - `ae`/`oe`/`ue`/`ss` are accepted, but you get a reminder to write the umlauts.
- **Synonyms:** if you type a different German word that also fits (e.g. *maßgeblich* when *ausschlaggebend* was wanted), you get another try instead of a mark against you.
- **Manual override:** if you think your answer was right, click **I was right**. If you guessed and got lucky, click **Count as wrong**.
- **Hint:** shows the first letters, e.g. `d__ A______`. A card answered with a hint is scored as "almost" at best.

## Adding words

- Use the **Words** tab to add, edit or delete words, or to paste many lines at once.
- You can also drop any `*.csv` file into `data/`. It is imported on the next start.

Format of each line (example, its English translation and rank are optional):

```
german;english;level;example;example_en
die Tragweite;scope|significance;C2;Die Tragweite ist enorm.;The implications are enormous.
```

- Put `|` between alternative answers.
- Write German nouns **with their article**.
- Level is `B2`, `C1` or `C2`.

## Word lists

- `data/words_b2_c2.csv`: 367 hand-picked C1/C2 words and idioms. These come first.
- `data/words_wiktionary.csv`: about 17,900 words generated by `tools/build_wordlist.py`.
  - The ~1500 most frequent (basic) words are skipped.
  - Frequency rank 1500–5000 is B2, 5000–11000 is C1 and 11000–20000 is C2.
  - New words are introduced roughly in order of frequency.
  - About 84% have an example sentence with an English translation.
  - Sources:
    - meanings: Wiktionary (CC BY-SA), via kaikki.org
    - frequencies: OpenSubtitles, via hermitdave/FrequencyWords
    - example sentences: Tatoeba (CC BY 2.0 FR) and Wiktionary
  - These entries are machine-generated, so some meanings are odd or incomplete. Use **Edit word** when you spot one.
